Let's start here

So, what is an objection?

YOUR TURN In the Teams chat, finish this sentence: "An objection is..."

Our definition

Click to reveal
Any reason a student gives for not taking the next step, like enrolling, staying in a unit or booking a call. It's a concern, not a decision.

A real barrier

"I'm working double shifts until March."
What it needs
A practical plan that works around it.

Missing information

"I didn't know there was any support."
What it needs
Clear, honest facts. Often that's all it takes.

A feeling

"I'm just not smart enough for this."
What it needs
Acknowledgement first. Facts won't land until they feel heard.

A stall

"I'll think about it."
What it needs
Gentle exploring to find what's really underneath.

Work out which type it is before you respond. That tells you which tool to reach for.

  • Give the chat 20 seconds to finish the sentence. Read out two or three, then click to reveal our definition.
  • For each type, read the example and ask "What would this student need from us?" before clicking.
  • Land the point: the same words ("I'm too busy") can be any of these four types. That's why we explore before we answer.

The backbone

LAER: the shape of every objection

LAER stands for Listen, Acknowledge, Explore, Respond. It's a simple four-step model used widely in sales and service. The point: don't answer an objection until you understand it.

L

Listen

Let them finish. Don't plan your answer while they talk.
Pause. Note their exact words.
A

Acknowledge

Show you heard the concern before you respond to it.
"That makes sense, it's a lot to juggle."
E

Explore

Ask questions to find the real concern underneath.
"What's making this term feel so full?"
R

Respond

Only now, answer what you found and agree a next step.
"Let's map week one to your weekends."

LAER in 30 seconds: "It's just too expensive."

LSSA(lets them finish, stays quiet for a moment)
ASSA"That's fair. It's a big investment, and it makes sense to think it through."
ESSA"Can I ask, is it the overall cost, or the timing of the payment that's hard right now?"
EStudent"Honestly, it's the timing. Things are tight until March."
RSSA"That helps. Have you looked at FEE-HELP? If you're eligible, it covers your fees so there's nothing to pay up front, and you repay through the tax system once you earn over the threshold. I'll send you the details today."

Where do the other tools fit?

Click to reveal
Acknowledge: Labelling, Feel and Felt. Explore: Isolate and clarify, GROW Goal and Reality. Respond: Found, Reframe, GROW Options and Way forward.
  • What it is: LAER (Listen, Acknowledge, Explore, Respond) is a long-standing objection-handling model from sales and customer service. It's popular because it's easy to remember on a live call.
  • LAER is the order. Every other tool today plugs into one of these four steps.

Hand-off to for GROW
Explore and Respond tool: GROW

GROW, applied to objections

GROW stands for Goal, Reality, Options, Way forward. It's one of the best-known coaching models. Instead of telling, you ask questions that help the student think it through and reach their own decision.

G

Goal

Reconnect the student to why they started and what they want.
"What made you start the program?"
"Where do you want to be by the end of the year?"
R

Reality

Get the facts behind the objection. Listen more than you talk.
"What's making this term feel so full?"
"What have you tried so far?"
O

Options

Let the student generate possible paths before you suggest any.
"What could make this more manageable?"
"What else could you try?"
W

Way forward

Agree one clear next step the student owns, with a check-in.
"What's your first step, and when?"
"When shall we touch base?"

The student owns it

A plan they came up with sticks far better than one we hand them.

It finds the real concern

Reality questions get past the first thing they say to what's actually in the way.

It ends with a commitment

Every GROW conversation finishes with a specific next step and a check-in.

It's a guide, not a script. You might start at Reality and loop back to the Goal, and that's fine.

  • What it is: GROW was developed in the UK in the 1980s by coaches including Sir John Whitmore. TLs use it in our own coaching sessions, so many of you will recognise it.
  • Today we use it on the phone with students: in LAER terms, Goal and Reality are how we Explore, and Options and Way forward are how we Respond.
  • Key shift: we ask, the student answers. Questions do the persuading, not us.
  • Ask: "Which stage do we usually skip?" (Answer is usually Reality: we jump straight to Options.)

Hand-off to for Feel, Felt, Found
Acknowledge and Respond tool

Feel, Felt, Found

Feel

Acknowledge the emotion. Name it, don't fix it.
"That makes complete sense...""I can hear how frustrating..."

Felt

Normalise it. They are not the only one.
"Lots of students have felt...""You're not alone in that..."

Found

Share what genuinely helped others.
"What they found was...""What helped others was..."
Student"I'm thinking about dropping out. I'm so behind."
Feel"I can hear how stressful being behind feels, especially with everything else going on."
Felt"A lot of students have felt exactly the same around this point in the term."
Found"What many found was that focusing on just the next assessment, with a simple catch-up plan, made it feel doable again."

Co-host move: reads the student line, gives the response.

  • Emphasise the pause after Feel. Let it land before moving on.
  • The Found must be real. If you don't know what helped others, ask a Reality question instead.
Feel, Felt, Found

When to use it, and when not to

Use it when

  • The objection is emotional: overwhelm, confidence, worry about cost
  • The student is venting and needs to feel heard first
  • You want to open the door before moving into GROW

Avoid it when

  • The student asked a direct factual question ("When is census?")
  • They've made an informed decision and want to stop
  • It would sound scripted or rushed
  • You'd have to invent a "Found"

Make it sound like you

Vary the words. Use the student's own language back to them. Never say "I know exactly how you feel".
  • Ask: "Has anyone had FFF backfire?" Take one story.
  • Stress the ethics point: a Found is always true and never a promise.

Quick tools

Three more tools for your belt

Labelling

Name the emotion you hear, then pause and let them talk.
"It sounds like...""It seems like..."

Isolate and clarify

Check it's the real, and only, concern before you answer it.
"Apart from that, is there anything else?""If we could sort that out, would you feel okay to continue?"

Reframe

Offer a new way to see the concern, without arguing with it.
"Another way to look at it...""What if..."

Labelling example

Click to reveal
Student: "I just don't know if I can keep doing this."
SSA: "It sounds like you're feeling pretty worn out right now." (pause)

Isolate example

Click to reveal
Student: "It's the cost."
SSA: "Apart from the cost, is there anything else making you hesitate?"

Reframe example

Click to reveal
Student: "I'm too busy to study."
SSA: "That's exactly when one unit can work: it keeps your progress moving while life is busy, so you're not starting from scratch later."
  • Read each tool card, then click the matching example box underneath to reveal it. can read the student lines.
  • Labelling: the pause matters more than the words. Count to three in your head.
  • Isolating stops us answering the first objection when the real one is the second.
  • A reframe only works after Acknowledge. Too early, it sounds like arguing.

Hand-off to for the lightning round
Lightning round

True or false?

YOUR TURN Type T (true) or F (false) in the Teams chat as fast as you can

Start the round, read each statement, click the chat's majority answer, read the reason in one sentence and move on. Keep the energy high. Two minutes on the clock.

Answers:

  1. FFF should be read word for word as a script. False: use the structure, not the exact words.
  2. In LAER, you Respond before you Explore. False: explore first.
  3. Isolating means asking "Apart from that, is there anything else?" before you respond. True
  4. Reframing means telling the student their concern is wrong. False: it adds a new view, never argues.
  5. A Found should only include outcomes you can genuinely stand behind. True
  6. Way forward means the SSA decides the next step. False: agree it together.
  7. When labelling, you name the emotion and then pause. True
  8. If a student says "please stop calling", keep pushing with FFF. False: respect the boundary.

Wrap up

What we'll take onto the phones

  • An objection is information, not rejection
  • LAER is the order: Listen, Acknowledge, Explore, Respond
  • Labelling, Feel and Felt to acknowledge
  • Isolate and GROW to find the real concern
  • Found, Reframe and a Way forward to respond
  • Every Found is honest. Every next step is agreed.
